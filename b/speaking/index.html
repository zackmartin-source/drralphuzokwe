About

Built it, lost some,
built again.

Nigeria raised me, America tested me, and thirty plus ventures taught me the one thing that never appears in a business plan: comfort kills faster than competition.

I studied at night, worked through the day, and put whatever was left into the first business, then the next one. Some worked in a month. Some took years. A few closed, and those taught me the most, because a business that dies tells you exactly where you stopped paying attention.

Speaking

Bring me to your room.

Boardrooms, congregations, associations, universities and conferences. I speak from thirty years of building businesses across four countries, a doctorate in business strategy, and seven books written on the same question: what does a person actually do on Monday morning.

01

Change, Adapt, or Die

Sixty years of business history, told through the giants who saw the change coming and stood still. Kodak invented the digital camera. Blockbuster laughed a young Netflix out of the room. The room leaves with the six habits that show up in every survivor, and one uncomfortable question about its own industry.

02

Adaptation Is Not Surrender

The talk for a workforce that hears the word change and hears the word loss. Where the fear comes from, why studying what is emerging beats mourning what is disappearing, and how a team decides which of its habits are principles and which are only habits.

03

Leadership Begins Before You Lead Anyone

Can you hold your attitude when the quarter is bad, stay disciplined when nobody is watching, and take responsibility in public for a decision that cost you. Influence is not control, it is connection, and this session takes a room through the difference in their own working relationships.

04

Activate Your Greatness

Most people are not short of potential, they are short of a system. Belief, focus, the body that has to carry the vision, and the quiet that lets a person hear their own direction again. Practical, daily, and measurable by the end of the week.

05

Wealth That Survives You

Why most fortunes do not reach a third generation, what separates an owner from a consumer, and the trap that catches people the year after their income doubles. Built on real cases, including families who lost everything inside three generations.

06

Living with Artificial Intelligence

Seventy years from Turing to the machine on your desk, without the hysteria. What has genuinely changed in medicine, in offices and in classrooms, which work is moving, and the sentence that matters most: it will not replace you, but someone using it might.
